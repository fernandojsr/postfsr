Key Insights
Europe

A Tale of Two Europes

Northern Europe (Sweden, Germany, Netherlands, Denmark) employs 73–77% of workers aged 55–64. Southern Europe (Italy, Greece, Romania) struggles to exceed 55–60%. Germany leads the DACH region with 35 of 38 regions already meeting the EU's 2030 employment target.

AI Adoption

The Generational AI Gap

50% of Gen Z and Millennials have used generative AI, versus only 22% of Baby Boomers. In the EU, older workers and women are specifically identified as the least likely to deploy AI at work — a gap Cedefop and the EU Commission have formally committed to closing.

Skills Gap

Willing but Undertrained

70% of workers over 45 expect AI to transform their roles within three years, yet nearly half have received no employer-sponsored training. Companies' AI training budgets skew dramatically toward employees under 40 — leaving experienced talent exposed.

Workforce Risk

120 Million at Risk

The WEF projects that 59 in every 100 global workers will need reskilling by 2030 — 11 of whom are unlikely to receive it, translating to over 120 million workers at medium-term redundancy risk. Experienced workers without AI literacy are disproportionately vulnerable.

Why Hire Senior Talent

Experience doesn't expire.
It compounds.

In a labour market increasingly disrupted by AI, there is a reflexive temptation to equate youth with adaptability and experience with obsolescence. The data tells a different story — and so does business reality.

Senior professionals bring what no algorithm can generate: contextual judgement. Decades of navigating organisational complexity, managing crises, leading teams through change, and delivering results across economic cycles produce a form of intelligence that is irreplaceable. They have seen trends before they were called trends.

Their institutional knowledge is a strategic asset. Research consistently shows that experienced workers improve team decision-making quality, reduce costly errors, and accelerate the development of junior colleagues. A 2024 Brookings study found that workers who combined AI upskilling with existing experience showed 27% higher job retention rates — precisely because experience amplifies the value of new tools, rather than competing with them.

The AI moment makes this more urgent, not less. Deploying AI effectively in real business environments — healthcare, manufacturing, public administration, education — requires deep domain knowledge. You cannot prompt your way to competence without understanding the domain. Senior professionals who acquire AI literacy become exponentially more powerful, not redundant.

There is also a human-centred argument. As AI handles increasing volumes of data processing and routine analysis, human skills — empathy, ethical reasoning, stakeholder communication, cross-cultural leadership — rise in relative value. These are precisely the capabilities refined over long careers.

Hiring senior professionals with AI skills is not a social gesture. It is a competitive advantage. The organisations that understand this first will benefit most.
